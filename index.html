Putting AI to work on heat transfer

We are a working group of 40 researchers from universities, companies, and national laboratories. We organize the conference sessions, webinars, benchmarks, and community roadmap that bring AI into thermal science and engineering.

Submit to the special issue

Founded 2026 · Coordinated by Tengfei Luo, University of Notre Dame

Mission

The group exists to grow the use of AI across heat transfer research, education, and engineering practice. We build the venues and resources that connect the thermal community with AI, serving researchers who develop new algorithms for thermal problems as well as those who simply want to use AI tools well in their everyday work.

  1. Conferences. An AI track and topics for conferences, panels, tutorials and short courses, and a teaching panel.
  2. Webinars. A quarterly speaker series that mixes domain experts, AI specialists, and industry voices, starting fall 2026.
  3. A community roadmap. A paper on where AI in heat transfer stands and where it can go, from emerging tools to long-standing problems AI might finally unlock.
  4. Benchmarks and education. Shared datasets and data guidelines, an annual competition, and activities that help students build AI skills.

Subgroups

Four subgroups carry out the group's activities. Each runs its own meetings and welcomes new contributors.

News

Jul 28, 2026

Working group kicks off at SHTC 2026

The group held its hybrid kickoff meeting in Bellevue, WA, with 35 participants from academia, industry, and national laboratories. The group adopted the name "AI for Heat Transfer," confirmed its first-year priorities (a community roadmap paper, a quarterly webinar series, and an AI conference track), and welcomed 15 new members.

Jul 26, 2026

Journal special issue opens for submissions

The Journal of Heat and Mass Transfer opened a special issue on AI and machine learning for thermal science and engineering, guest-edited by five researchers including three group members. Details and the call for papers are in the Special issue section.
